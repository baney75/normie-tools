## What this is

The IRS has a free way to do your taxes online. It's called **IRS Free File**. Many people who could use it never find it, or they end up on a website that charges them.

NT's Form Filler walks you through it one small step at a time, on your phone or your computer. It gets your answers ready. **You** stay in charge: you make the account, you sign, and you press Submit.

## Step 1: Check if this is for you

Answer these four questions.

1. **Are you filing this year's taxes?** Free File only does the current year. It can't do taxes from past years.
2. **How much did you make last year?** The "guided" Free File was free for people whose income was **$95,000 or less** last season. The IRS calls this number your *adjusted gross income*, or AGI. It's on line 11a of the 2025 tax form. NT can help you find it.
3. **Made more than that?** You can still file for free with **Free File Fillable Forms**. It works at any income, but you do more of the work yourself.
4. **Is it open yet?** Free File is closed right now. It opens again on IRS.gov in **January 2027**. The IRS has said Free File will keep running through October 2029.

**To confirm, posted in January:** the income limit for 2027 and the list of companies taking part. Each company also sets its own rules, such as age or state. NT will show you the real numbers once the IRS posts them.

**What about Direct File?** The IRS's Direct File isn't offered, and the IRS hasn't set any date to bring it back. NT doesn't use it.

**When to get free help from a person instead:** NT sends you to free help at a VITA site if your taxes aren't simple. That includes a business with many costs, a home sale, income from another country, or a letter from the IRS. At VITA sites, trained volunteers do your taxes for free. Many sites help people who make about $70,244 or less. For people 60 and older there's a sister program called TCE. Find a site with the IRS VITA locator or by calling 800-906-9887.

## Step 2: Gather your papers

NT makes this checklist for you. Tick each one off as you find it.

- [ ] **Social Security number** (or ITIN) for you and for everyone on your return, like a spouse or children
- [ ] **W-2** from each job (your employer sends it in January or February)
- [ ] **1099 forms** for other money you got. For example:
  - 1099-NEC for gig or freelance work
  - 1099-K from payment apps or online selling
  - 1099-G for unemployment
  - 1099-INT for bank interest
  - 1099-R for a pension or retirement money
  - SSA-1099 for Social Security
- [ ] **1095-A** if you had health insurance from the Marketplace
- [ ] **Last year's tax return**, if you filed one. You need last year's income number (AGI) or the 5-digit PIN you picked last year.
- [ ] **Your IP PIN**, only if the IRS sent you one
- [ ] **Your bank account and routing numbers**, if you want your refund sent straight to your bank
- [ ] Papers for credits, if you have them:
  - child care costs
  - school costs (Form 1098-T from the school)
  - money you put into a retirement account
- [ ] For side jobs: what you were paid, and receipts for what you spent

Don't have last year's income number? You can see it by signing in to your IRS online account, under Tax Records. You can also ask the IRS to mail you a copy of last year's return (called a transcript).

You can take phone photos of your papers. NT reads them on your phone or computer and doesn't send them anywhere.

## Step 3: Pick the right free way

NT asks you a few questions, then suggests one of these. You always make the final choice.

| | Guided Free File | Free File Fillable Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Who can use it | People under last season's income limit ($95,000); each company sets its own extra rules | Anyone, at any income |
| How it feels | Simple questions, one at a time. The software does the math. | Online copies of the IRS forms. You type into the boxes, with only some math done for you. |
| State taxes | Some companies do your state return for free, some charge | Not included |
| Best for | Most people | People over the limit, or people who know the forms |

**Always start at IRS.gov.** The IRS says you only get Free File if you start at IRS.gov/freefile. If you go straight to a tax company's own website, you won't get the free deal.

## Watch out for these

- **Extra charges.** Under Free File rules, companies can't sell you extras on a free return. That includes add-on services, hidden fees, refund loans, and "audit protection". A company may still charge for a **state** return, or if you don't meet its rules. If any screen asks for a card number, **stop**. NT tells you why and helps you find a free choice.
- **Fake or look-alike sites.** Only use the IRS.gov link that NT's Form Finder gives you. Don't click tax links in texts, emails, or ads. NT's Scam Check can look at a link for you.
- **Someone asking for money to "get your refund faster".** That isn't part of Free File.

## Step 5: Credits people often miss

A credit lowers your tax, and some credits can give you money back even if you owe no tax. NT **checks** for these. It doesn't decide for you: it shows the rule, and you confirm.

- **Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC).** For people who work and earn a low to moderate income. For tax year 2025 it was up to $649 with no children, and up to $8,046 with 3 or more children. Your income had to be under a limit, for example $19,104 for a single person with no children, or $57,310 for a single parent with 2 children. **Starting with tax year 2026, claiming this credit also needs a new form, Schedule 3-A** (details to confirm in January). The IRS EITC Assistant can check if you qualify.
- **Child Tax Credit.** For children under 17 who lived with you more than half the year, and each needs a valid Social Security number. For tax year 2025 it was up to $2,200 per child. If you owe little or no tax, up to $1,700 per child can come back to you, as long as you earned at least $2,500.
- **Saver's Credit.** If you put money into a retirement account (like an IRA or a 401(k) at work), you may get back 10% to 50% of up to $2,000 ($4,000 for a married couple). You must be 18 or older, not a full-time student, and not claimed on someone else's return.
- **Other checks NT runs:** credit for child care costs, credit for school costs (from your 1098-T), and the credit for other dependents ($500 for a dependent who doesn't qualify for the Child Tax Credit).

The 2026 amounts get posted by the IRS before filing season. NT uses the numbers for the year you're filing (to confirm in January).

If your case is complicated, NT stops and points you to free help from a VITA volunteer. For example: you're not sure who can claim a child, or you were self-employed with many costs.

## Step 6: After you file

1. **Confirmation.** You get an email once the IRS accepts your return. NT saves a copy for you. If your return is rejected, you can usually fix it and send it again before the deadline. NT helps you read why.
2. **Track your refund.** Use the IRS **"Where's My Refund?"** tool at IRS.gov or in the IRS app. You can check 24 hours after you file. Most refunds sent online arrive about 3 weeks after you file.
3. **If you claimed the EITC or the extra Child Tax Credit,** the law says the IRS can't send your refund before mid-February, even if you file early.
4. **Your refund may be smaller** if you owe back taxes or certain other debts, like child support. The government mails you a letter if that happens.
5. **Keep your copy.** NT keeps your answer sheet and confirmation in your Tax Folder, so next year is quicker.

## For builders

- **Fillable Forms is web screens, not a PDF.** Free File Fillable Forms is an online app with its own screens and checks. For example, negative numbers need a minus sign, you can't attach statements, and Form 1040-SR isn't supported (use Form 1040). Form Filler's PDF path doesn't apply here. Instead, NT makes an **answer sheet** (`fill-sheet.json`, plus a PDF) laid out screen by screen. Each value cites its source paper and the official instruction line.
- **Guided Free File is a partner company's website.** Each partner has its own screen flow, so NT can't fill screens it doesn't know. Default: show the person a values table matched to IRS form lines. Screen-by-screen maps only for partners we've recorded and tested.
- **Assisted filling: to confirm.** Do the partner sites' terms allow a browser agent (an AI helper that uses websites for the person) to type answers? Do the IRS's Fillable Forms terms allow it? Until both are checked, the default is that **the person types**, with NT showing the screen and value side by side.
- **Prepare and stop.** The agent flow ends on the review page. Before the Sign or Submit step, the engine adds a hard stop that no setting can turn off. NT never stores the Free File password, the self-select PIN, or the IP PIN. The person types them each time.
- **Upsell guard.** Watch for page text and fields such as "card number", "upgrade", "audit defense", "refund advance", or a price. When one shows up, stop and explain in plain words.
- **Official links only.** Form Finder serves `IRS.gov/freefile` and the IRS VITA locator from the official-site list. No partner URL is ever shown unless it came from the IRS.gov partner list that same day.
- **The tax math lives in tested code.** The model labels and asks questions. It never computes a line. See test-design.md, section 11, for the zero-tolerance tax suite.
- **Yearly reset.** Each January, rebuild the rule tables from the new IRS instructions and tables, and re-run the full suite before the tool says "ready for this year".
