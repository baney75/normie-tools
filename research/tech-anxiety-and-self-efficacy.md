# Tech anxiety and self-efficacy

Why it matters for NT: people who doubt they "can do computers" quit early. Preview, undo, and plain errors build confidence.

## Sources

1. Wild et al. "Computer-related self-efficacy and anxiety in older adults with and without mild cognitive impairment." Alzheimer's and Dementia, 2012.
   - URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalz.2011.12.008
   - Finding: Training and real computer use change computer self-efficacy and anxiety scores in older adults; effects differ with mild cognitive impairment.
   - Checked: 2026-11-05
   - Note: Abstract retrieved via EuropePMC API for PMC3483565.

2. Nielsen Norman Group. "Minimize Cognitive Load to Maximize Usability."
   - URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/
   - Finding: Designs that match familiar mental models cut the learning burden that often feeds tech anxiety.
   - Checked: 2026-11-05
